I DOLook closer · 8 min

Different properties.
Different ways to separate.

A mixture contains substances that are physically combined. We can separate them by taking advantage of a difference in their properties.

S

Sand is insoluble

Sand does not dissolve in water. Its solid grains remain separate and are large enough to be trapped by ordinary filter paper.

NaCl

Salt is soluble

Salt dissolves in water. The dissolved particles are dispersed through the liquid and pass through ordinary filter paper with the water.

H₂O

Water can evaporate

Water changes into a gas and leaves the solution. The salt remains behind and crystals form as enough water is removed.

TEACHER THINK-ALOUD

“I match the method to the property.”

“First, I need to remove an insoluble solid. Filtration traps the sand, but the salt solution passes through. Then I can evaporate water from that solution to recover the salt.”

What if I also need the water? Use distillation: evaporate water, then cool its vapour to condense and collect liquid water. Evaporation on its own does not collect the water.

Words to use in your explanation
Solute
The substance dissolved in a solution, such as salt.
Solvent
The liquid that dissolves a solute, such as water.
Residue
The material left on the filter paper.
Filtrate
The liquid that passes through the filter paper.

04 · SEQUENCE

Choose the action for each stage.

Optional teacher-led practical: compare the model with real evidence

Equipment: prepared sand–salt–water mixture, beakers, funnel, filter paper, wash bottle and a labelled evaporation dish.

  1. Wear eye protection. Check that the mixture contains only the supplied substances.
  2. Filter slowly, keeping the residue and the filtrate.
  3. Rinse the sand residue with a small amount of clean water and allow it to dry.
  4. Transfer the filtrate to an evaporation dish. Leave it in a safe, labelled location to evaporate over time, or observe a teacher-managed heating demonstration under the school’s risk assessment.
  5. Record what is visible now and at the next observation.

No unsupervised heating. Hot equipment needs cooling time. Do not taste any sample. Dispose of materials as directed by your teacher.

TEACHING GUIDE

Run the pilot lesson

45 minutes · Year 7 · Chemical Sciences

Use the six sections for I Do → We Do → You Do instruction. Pause for mini-whiteboard predictions before revealing feedback. The model is a conceptual comparison, not a measured experiment.

  1. Launch (5 min): elicit the misconception that clear means pure.
  2. I Do (8 min): model the property → method → result reasoning.
  3. We Do (15 min): compare the three model samples and construct the separation sequence together.
  4. You Do (10 min): students transfer the reasoning to iron filings and recovering water.
  5. Exit (7 min): review explanations and collect exported response records.

Written response: suggested 3-point rubric

  • Sand is insoluble; its grains are trapped on the filter as residue (1).
  • Dissolved salt passes through the filter with water into the filtrate (1).
  • Evaporating water from the filtrate leaves salt; evaporation alone does not collect liquid water (1).
Show a model explanation

Sand does not dissolve in water. Its grains are trapped by the filter paper. Dissolved salt passes through the paper with the water, so the filtrate is still salt water. Evaporating water from this filtrate leaves the salt behind. If we need to collect the water as well, we can condense its vapour using distillation.

Support and extension

Support: open the vocabulary list and use the sentence starters. Extension: ask students to compare salt recovery by evaporation with collecting water by distillation, including the energy and apparatus needed.
