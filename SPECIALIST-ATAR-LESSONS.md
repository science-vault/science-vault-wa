# Mathematics Specialist ATAR: student lesson course

## Scope

91 focused lessons: 42 Year 11 and 49 Year 12. 1,385 screens; 557 original written tasks, including 182 worked examples, 375 independent tasks and 11 extended multi-part tasks. Each lesson contains explicit theory, an explained visual model, two worked examples, independent practice, suggested marking points and a concept check. Four exploration screens provide repeated-sampling/coverage or Euler activities. There are 50 distinct native SVG models and graphs.

Student entry: [Mathematics Specialist](mathematics-specialist.html). Choose Year 11 or Year 12, then a unit and a lesson tile in the topic hub. Mathematics Methods is assumed alongside Specialist; relevant prerequisite knowledge needs revision when indicated.

Mapping is accompanied by lessons and questions, rather than a claim that attaching an outcome number alone proves sufficient teaching. Current and published 2027 Year 12 statements were compared: the numbered sets match. Text extraction differences include mathematical fonts, missing bars/superscripts, page furniture and layout; the affected content was reviewed. This release uses the current teaching syllabus and does not substitute a future syllabus merely because it is published.

Principal complex argument uses −π < Arg(z) ≤ π, with zero excluded. Logistic parameters are distinguished between rP(K−P) and kP(1−P/K). Mean confidence intervals use the course's approximate large-sample normal interval with s/√n. Individual normal-value intervals use s and target a different quantity. Simulations include varied populations and sample sizes, and standardised sample means using estimated standard error. Normal approximation assumptions and limitations are explained.

The 2026 examination covers specify calculator-free: 50 working minutes, 48 raw marks, 35%; calculator-assumed: 100 working minutes, 86 raw marks, 65%. Reading times are 5 and 10 minutes respectively. Raw marks and section percentages are distinct. The 2025 examination report informed attention to domain restrictions, implicit second derivatives, geometric reasoning, distance versus displacement and interpretation of rate and interval results.

## Validation

Independent symbolic/numerical verification checks matrix products and inverses, systems, vector projections and cross products, complex arithmetic and factors, antiderivatives, definite integrals, areas and volumes, differential-equation solutions, SHM initial conditions and sample-size calculations. Rendering checks parse all TeX to native MathML. SVG text bounds are checked in the browser. Full course-player checks cover every screen and written task, reveal answers, unanswered/correct checks, draft persistence and self-credit, mobile width, simulation controls and all 91 hub tiles. Existing Chemistry, Psychology and Human Biology are checked after integration.

Written answers are self-assessed against suggested points; self-scores are not teacher-verified marks. Screen visits do not establish mastery. A single lesson concept check is a limited check of its central idea, not a substitute for written exam preparation. Browser-local draft/progress storage is distinct from a central account system.
