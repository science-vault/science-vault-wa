# Mathematics Methods ATAR: student lessons and coverage audit

Checked 8 October 2026. These lessons are original student learning resources. Suggested practice marks support self-review and are not official SCSA marking keys.

- 39 Year 11 lessons; 42 Year 12 lessons.
- 1,245 screens; 509 written tasks: 162 worked examples and 347 independent tasks, including 23 extended multi-part tasks.
- 41 precise SVG models with explanatory captions and limits.
- Repeated sampling of Bernoulli, uniform and normal populations; proportion standardisation and confidence capture; adjustable rectangle sums for integration.
- 81 formative concept checks. The separate mastery view repeats the lesson's central concept check; passing it alone does not establish ATAR readiness.

Each lesson provides prerequisites and goals, four or more focused explanations, an explained visual, two worked attempts with separate step-by-step solutions, independent practice with revealable marking points, a concept check and official practice links. Written answers are self-assessed; they are not automatically graded. Drafts and local progress save in the current browser. Existing lesson-tracking hooks are retained; this release does not create central student accounts.

## Teaching choices

- Function domains remain visible through translations, scaling, composition and equation solving.
- First-principles derivatives, angle-sum identities, the product rule and fundamental theorem include reasoning rather than only rules.
- Trig calculus uses radians; linear inner factors multiply in differentiation and divide in integration.
- Geometric area differs from signed accumulation; distance differs from displacement and final position.
- Bernoulli, binomial, density, CDF, expectation, variance and linear transformations are distinguished.
- Normal notation uses variance as its second parameter; device inputs may instead require SD.
- Proportion sampling SD uses model p; interval SE estimates use sample p-hat.
- Approximate Wald intervals require adequate successes/failures and defensible sampling. Extreme samples can have poor coverage; simulations display that limitation.
- Confidence describes repeated-interval coverage of a fixed parameter. A compatible claim is not proved true.
- Sample-size design rounds up and distinguishes interval width from margin; larger samples do not automatically remove bias.

All 3,909 mathematical expressions compiled strictly to browser-native MathML. Structural checks validated every lesson, question, allocation, visual and syllabus ID. Forty-four independent symbolic/numerical checks covered original fixed answers and representative models. Browser checks traversed all 1,245 screens, 509 task views and 81 concept checks; validated answer reveals, incomplete/correct checks, escaped saved drafts and self-credit persistence, simulation controls, all course tiles and existing Specialist/science course opening. No JavaScript errors or page-wide overflow occurred at 390px. SVG text bounds were checked; charts deliberately scroll horizontally on small screens. The complete integrated hub rendered MathML without visible raw TeX.

Coverage mappings are an auditable index; students should still use official papers for additional timed practice. The short formative check does not replace written practice or external assessment.
