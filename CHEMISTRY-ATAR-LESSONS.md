# Chemistry ATAR focused student lessons

## Scope and student experience

70 focused lessons replace the previous 16 broad modules: 34 in Year 11 and 36 in Year 12. There are 1,097 screens, 252 written exam-style tasks (134 worked and 118 independent), 150 concept-check items, and approximately 40,525 words of theory. Each focused lesson includes three new topic-specific teaching screens, a new six-mark worked task, a new six-mark independent task and a formative check. The 140 new written tasks add 840 suggested practice marks.

All original teaching screens, reference tables, four interactive models, 112 written tasks and 80 concept checks are retained exactly once within their focused parent groups. Original question IDs are preserved. Original drafts and self-assessed marking credit migrate from broad-module browser keys into the focused lesson when first opened. Old visited-screen indices and mastery passes are not imported as proof of completing the expanded lessons.

Lessons have 11–24 screens, sized around a coherent topic rather than padded to a fixed quota. Students study the theory and explained models, attempt the worked questions before the next-screen solution, then reveal marking guidance after independent attempts. Every new task supplies explicit marking allocations. Suggested points accept valid equivalent chemistry; the material is original ATAR-style practice, not official SCSA questions or an endorsed textbook. Browser-saved drafts and self-assessed marks are not teacher-verified scores. Paper drawings are not automatically stored.

Year 11 retains atoms/evidence/periodicity, materials/separation/nanotechnology, hydrocarbons, amounts/energy, molecular structure/interactions, chromatography/gases, aqueous chemistry and rates/inquiry across Units 1–2. Year 12 retains equilibrium, acids/buffers, titration, redox/electrochemistry and organic structures/polymers/proteins/synthesis across Units 3–4. Inquiry and science in society are integrated rather than confined to a single revision lesson. Repository Kennedy PowerPoints for atomic structure, materials, organic chemistry, intermolecular forces/gases and rates, and Safety Bay acids/bases support the original explanations; older conventions are checked against current official publications.

The 2026 exam has 10 minutes reading and 3 hours working: multiple choice 25 raw marks/25%, short answer 72 raw marks/35%, extended answer 94 raw marks/40%. Section weights are not interchangeable with raw marks. Suggested section times are 50, 60 and 70 minutes respectively. These facts do not turn the original learning tasks into official questions.

Focused explanations address examiner-report weaknesses: explicit species-specific intermolecular forces; correct organic connectivity and representations; atom AND charge balancing; suitable reaction arrows; observations distinguished from inferred identities; complete numerical substitutions, units and final rounding; Le Châtelier prediction distinguished from collision-based explanation; and corrosion protection tied to conditions.

Numerical conventions include SCSA STP at 0.0 °C and 100.0 kPa with 22.71 L mol⁻¹, compatible PV=nRT units, ppm on a mass-of-solution basis, and Kw/pH+pOH assumptions at 25 °C. Quantitative extensions and idealised models identify assumptions, including theoretical completion versus actual Haber equilibrium yield, reaction quotient versus K, and strong-acid titration versus weak-species hydrolysis. Advanced examples support the course without being represented as additional prescribed SCSA outcomes.

## Models and verification

128 distinct explained native SVG/chemical models appear in 131 figure placements. Each has a reading explanation and a limit. Six new focused models explain ion hydration, reaction extent, buffer response, acidic redox atom/charge audits, peptide connectivity and compounding stage yields. Four retained interactive calculations cover gas pressure, limiting reagent, equilibrium quotient and strong-acid titration. The model registry includes all original model entries. Total suggested written marks: 1332.

`node chemistry-atar-check.cjs` checks course integrity, question allocations, references, original-content preservation, coverage terms and model use. Development browser checks render all 1,097 screens at desktop and 390px mobile width, exercise every lesson through integrated hub navigation, saved drafts, parent-draft migration, answer reveal, marking credit, formative feedback and mastery. They independently test all four interactive numerical relationships and check all SVG text bounds. Representative visible mobile and desktop screenshots are inspected. No page errors or document overflow are accepted.

The tested player reuses the focused Psychology student workflow while retaining Chemistry’s scientific interactive functions and browser storage namespace. The hub is based on the current Psychology release and only Chemistry asset cache versions change to 2026.119, preserving the latest Human Biology, Physics and Psychology integrations.
